Module 02

First-order frequency response

G(s) = 1/(τs + 1) · M = 1/√(1 + (ωτ)²) · φ = −atan(ωτ). Unit gain, unit-amplitude sinusoid, steady state.

Check the explanation

Larger τ reduces M and increases the magnitude of the phase lag. The cutoff moves to a lower frequency.

Module 03

Second-order response & state space

m x″ + b x′ + kx = F · ωₙ = √(k/m) · ζ = b/(2√(km)). Zero initial displacement and velocity. A force step F = k N gives a final displacement of 1 m.

Presets use m = 1 kg and k = 4 N/m; change b to compare damping cases.

Check the explanation

Underdamped systems overshoot and spiral toward equilibrium. Critical and overdamped systems do not overshoot; their paths approach equilibrium without spiraling. Excess damping slows the response.

Metrics are measured from the sampled response: rise = 10–90%; settling = last departure from the ±2% band. Peak time is shown only for an oscillatory response.

State-space model

x₁ = displacement, x₂ = velocity · ẋ = Ax + BF · y = [1 0]x, D = 0

The phase trajectory starts at (0, 0) and approaches (1, 0). Hover over the path to inspect time.
